## 1 · Where du Liban stands today

The good news: when someone searches for **"du Liban"**, your new website is the
first result, and your Google listing has **4.4 stars with 1,180 reviews** — more
reviews than any similar restaurant nearby.

The bad news, and it matters for advertising:

- When people search **"halal restaurant Madrid"**, **"shisha terrace Madrid"** or
  even **"restaurant La Moraleja"**, du Liban simply **does not appear** — not in the
  map results, not anywhere. Restaurants in your own building do appear.
- On searches for your own name, Google currently shows your Tripadvisor page with
  the quote *"El peor restaurante árabe de Madrid…"* ("the worst Arab restaurant in
  Madrid") and a price label of €€€€ — both from an outdated, unclaimed profile.
- Your Google listing still describes the old business: a delivery-platform menu,
  delivery messaging, and no mention of halal, shisha, the terraces or private
  events. We covered this in detail in the local-SEO plan we sent you.

**Why this matters here:** ads bring people to your door, but the door is your
Google listing and your website. If a paid click lands on a listing that talks about
delivery and shows another company's menu, part of that money is wasted. Our strong
recommendation is to run the local-SEO fixes **first or in parallel** — most of them
are quick and free.

## 2 · Is the competition already advertising? (Yes.)

We checked live Google searches from Madrid and Google's Ads Transparency Center on
21 August:

- **Al-Mounia** (Moroccan restaurant, city centre) is running ads with the exact
  headline **"Restaurante Halal Madrid"** — they are paying to own the halal
  searches that should naturally belong to a certified 100% halal Lebanese kitchen.
- **Beytna** (Lebanese, 4.8★ · 5,400 reviews) runs sponsored placements on
  "restaurante libanés madrid".
- **TheFork** (the booking platform) also advertises on Lebanese searches — when
  they win the click, the diner chooses from a list of your competitors.
- **Liba Garden**: you may have seen their ad recently. On the day we checked, their
  ads were not running on any of the searches we tested — small advertisers switch
  campaigns on and off. They were **not** bidding on your name that day, and neither
  was anyone else. Casa Árabe, Daily and Áurea show no signs of advertising at all.

Honest note on their budgets: without paid spy-tools nobody outside those companies
knows exactly what they spend. Our informed estimate is that Al-Mounia and Beytna
each spend a few hundred euros per month (roughly €200–800, wide margin of error).
Once our own campaign runs, Google gives us exact overlap data about every
competitor for free ("Auction Insights") — after 2–3 weeks we will know precisely
who bids against us and how often they win.

**The takeaway:** the field is half-empty. Nobody combines halal + Lebanese + shisha
+ north Madrid. Right now, entering is cheap.

## 3 · What we propose

Search ads on Google, targeted at the north of Madrid (Alcobendas, La Moraleja,
Sanchinarro, Las Tablas, SS de los Reyes), shown during your opening hours, sending
people to your website and your reservation page. Six groups of searches, in order
of priority:

1. **Your own name** ("du liban", "du liban reservas"…) — costs cents, guarantees
   the top result is yours and not the negative Tripadvisor quote, and protects you
   the day a competitor decides to bid on your name.
2. **Lebanese restaurant** — "restaurante libanés madrid / alcobendas / la moraleja".
3. **Halal** — "restaurante halal madrid", "comida halal madrid". Highest-affinity
   audience; you have the certificate, the competitor advertising there doesn't
   even serve Lebanese food.
4. **Shisha** — "terraza shisha madrid", "cachimba madrid". Nobody is advertising
   there today.
5. **Local generic** — "restaurante la moraleja / alcobendas" (larger budgets only).
6. **Occasions** — romantic dinners north Madrid, private events.

## 4 · What should you spend? Three scenarios

We worked backwards from your business, not from a budget. An average table is
roughly **€90–110** (your €30–40 per person × 2.5–3 guests). Paying up to ~€15 in
advertising for a new booked table is comfortable; up to ~€25 is acceptable while
testing — especially since a happy local guest comes back without you paying again.

First, two numbers we need FROM YOU, because the whole profitability calculation
depends on them: **what is your real average ticket per table (not per person), and
roughly what margin do you keep per table?** We've assumed ~€100 per table based on
your €30–40 per person — if the real figure is different, the budgets below move
with it.

| | **Test** | **Standard** | **Aggressive** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly budget (ad spend) | **€300** | **€600** | **€1,200** |
| Search groups | Name + Lebanese + Halal | + Shisha + Events | Everything, wider area |
| Expected bookings per month* | 20–50 | 45–110 | needs wider targeting |
| Expected cost per booking* | €5–14 | €5–14 | €6–18 |

\* Estimates, not promises. The click prices are real data we pulled from Google's
Keyword Planner on 21 August: for our target searches they run **€0.10–0.85 per
click** — unusually cheap, because almost nobody is bidding in this niche yet. We
assume 5–8% of clicks become a booking. **The first trial month exists precisely to
replace these estimates with du Liban's real numbers** — after it we'll know the
true cost per booking and can adjust the budget up, down, or off.

A few real data points from that research: searches for "restaurante halal cerca de
mí" run 10,000–100,000 per month in Spain (we'd capture the north-Madrid slice);
"restaurante libanés madrid", "restaurante la moraleja" and "restaurante alcobendas"
each run 1,000–10,000 per month; and searches for your own name are growing fast
(+900% in three months for "duliban restaurante").

**Our recommendation: start with Test for two months, then move to Standard.**
There's a specific reason for that sequence:

**Your €400 Google Ads credit.** The offer in your Google panel is real, but read
the small print: it's for new advertisers only, you must **spend €400 of your own
money within 60 days first**, and the €400 credit is added afterwards and **expires
60 days after it's granted**. The Test scenario spends exactly that in two months —
so month 3 (Standard) is largely paid for by Google. We'll verify the exact terms
in your panel before activating anything.

One caveat on the Aggressive scenario: ads generate demand you must seat. If Friday
and Saturday nights are already full, we'd point the extra budget at quieter slots
(weekday lunches, Sunday evenings) — that's where an extra table is pure gain.

## 5 · What to expect, honestly

- **Week 1:** ads visible, clicks arriving. Google's system needs 2–4 weeks of
  learning before results stabilise — judging (or changing) anything earlier is
  counterproductive.
- **Month 1:** first real numbers: what a click costs *you*, what a booking costs
  *you*. We adjust keywords and drop what doesn't work.
- **Months 2–3:** stabilised cost per booking, credit applied, decision point on
  scaling up.
- **What ads will NOT do:** fix the outdated Google listing, improve your 4.4
  rating, or put you in the map results — that's the local-SEO work, which keeps
  paying after you stop paying for ads. Ads are the accelerator, not the engine.

## 6 · Measuring results properly (one-time setup)

Your bookings happen on SevenRooms' website, not yours — so out of the box, Google
can't tell which booking came from an ad. Three layers fix this:

1. **From day one, free:** call tracking (Google shows a forwarding number in ads —
   and since your phone is answered by the AI, every ad-driven call is captured),
   plus SevenRooms' own per-channel booking report.
2. **The proper fix:** SevenRooms can install our measurement container inside
   their booking page — it's a support request from your SevenRooms account. Then
   we count actual completed reservations per euro spent.
3. **The legal part (this is real work, not a formality):** your website currently
   uses no tracking at all, and its legal/cookies page proudly says so. Adding
   Google's measurement requires upgrading the cookie-consent banner to EU
   standards (Consent Mode v2) and rewriting the legal text so it stays true.
   That's roughly **11–17 hours of one-time technical work** on our side. We'd
   rather quote it now than surprise you later.

## 7 · And Tripadvisor?

**We recommend not paying Tripadvisor.** Reasons:

- Their ads send people to **your Tripadvisor profile**, not to your website or
  your booking page — and your Tripadvisor profile (4.0★, 390 reviews, wrong €€€€
  price label, old phone number) is currently your weakest showcase. Paying to
  promote it would amplify the weak profile instead of the strong one (Google:
  4.4★, 1,180 reviews).
- Tripadvisor's audience is mostly tourists planning trips; your business is local
  regulars in north Madrid.
- Pricing is opaque (quoted per-venue; references range roughly €90–360/month) and
  their paid products' ROI is widely questioned even in tourist-heavy hospitality.

What we WILL do with Tripadvisor is free: claim the profile, fix the price range,
phone and website, and add current photos — because that profile shows up when
people search your name.
